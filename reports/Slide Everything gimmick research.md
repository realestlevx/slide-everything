# Keep the Slide, Earn the "Everything"

The word "everything" won't decide whether this game works. Nothing shows that the word helps a Roblox game succeed. The two biggest games built on the "consume the world" fantasy, Eat the World (52.6K peak concurrent players) and Slide Down A Hill (25.4K), leave it out of their titles. The one "everything" breakout right now, Eat Everything In A Grocery Store (18.7K peak), took its whole title from a record-breaking MrBeast video. The real problem is smaller and you can fix it. Wherever "everything" has paid off, on Roblox or elsewhere, it meant either **a size ladder that ends at absurd scale** (Katamari goes from thumbtacks to stars) or **a finite set the player can finish** (a whole grocery store of 175 foods). Twelve hand-sized items on a rarity ladder give players neither. The slide is your real asset. **Kick a Lucky Block**, the 2026 hit closest in structure to your game (roll, place for income, train to go farther), **peaked at 1.69M players**. Plain "distance pays" games, slide games included, peak at about 1.7K-25K and fade to a few dozen players. The 2025-2026 hits stood apart through a nameable collectible set, staged rare pulls, stakes during the run, and weekly updates and events. Since June 2026, Roblox has ranked games on 28-day retention, with click-through and first-minute bounce as its top two signals. So your title has to win the click and also match what players see in the first minute. **My recommendation:** keep the game, the steering and the verb "Slide". Keep the name "Slide Everything" only if you make the word true: turn the rarity ladder into a scale ladder that ends with sliding a planet or the sun, add a Slide Index, themed tiers above Mythic and mutations, and give the slide choices and risk. If you won't build that, rename before launch to a title that names the loop, such as "Roll & Slide". A realistic target is a lasting small game with a few thousand players at its peak, not a brainrot-sized spike.

## "Everything" titles spike and fade, and the 634M-visit leader of the fantasy never used the word

Roblox has a long tail of "Everything" games, and their numbers say the word isn't doing the work. The table compares the main ones with the two biggest games built on the same fantasy that skip the word. All figures are from Rolimons, fetched 2026-10-23. Trackers disagree at the edges; RoWatcher, for example, puts Everything Upgrade Tree's peak at 3K, not 8.7K ([RoWatcher](https://rowatcher.com/games/6749892429/everything-upgrade-tree)).

| Game | All-time peak CCU | Activity in Oct 2026 | What "everything" meant |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Eat Everything In A Grocery Store](https://www.rolimons.com/game/128201586883489) | 18,656 (~Oct 22, 2026) | 12,857 online, still rising | A finite set: empty the store, with a 175-food index |
| [Everything Upgrade Tree](https://www.rolimons.com/game/122809141833750) | 8,718 (Aug 31, 2025) | 143 online; 30-day peak 402; 23-min sessions | A widening set of systems |
| [The Everything Factory](https://www.rolimons.com/game/96033111684913) | 7,360 (Jun 5, 2026) | 30-day peak 35 | Rare items on a tycoon conveyor |
| [Vacuum Everything](https://www.rolimons.com/game/84402061711337) | 1,565 (Jul 11, 2025) | 7-day peak 6 | Vacuuming blocks, crates |
| [Eat the World](https://www.rolimons.com/game/16480898254) (no "everything") | 52,619 (Mar 14, 2025) | 30-day peak 4,380; 634.7M visits | A size ladder: eat the world |
| [Slide Down A Hill](https://www.rolimons.com/game/13682880624) (no "everything") | 25,398 (Jul 17, 2023) | 30-day peak 60; 199M visits | Riding ever-sillier objects down a slide |

These games follow three paths. **Trend-jacks** borrow attention. MrBeast's "Eat Everything In A Grocery Store, Win $1,000,000" drew **70M+ views in its first 24 hours** after its Oct 3, 2026 upload ([Dexerto](https://www.dexerto.com/youtube/mrbeast-breaks-his-youtube-views-record-with-1-million-grocery-store-challenge-3415556/)). The Roblox adaptation jumped from 1,441 to 12,145 concurrent players in the 24 hours after its Oct 17 update ([RoStats](https://rostats.io/records)). **Spike-and-crash** is the most common path: The Everything Factory fell from 7,360 to a 30-day peak of 35 in about four months, and Vacuum Everything fell from 1,565 to 6. **Only Everything Upgrade Tree lasts**: 23-minute average sessions and still 100-400 concurrent players almost two years in. It's also the game closest to yours, an incremental where "everything" means an ever-growing list of systems ([Rolimons](https://www.rolimons.com/game/122809141833750)). Ratings follow depth, not the word: The Everything Factory has 92.6% likes, while the thinner Vacuum Everything has 70.2%.

I found no evidence that players punish the word. No Reddit or DevForum thread discusses it. General naming advice warns against crowded, generic and look-alike names, and notes that "once you have a lot of players, the gameplay matters more than the name" ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/advice-on-naming-a-game/1989821)). The clearest signal comes from YouTubers. They put "EVERYTHING" in capitals in video titles about games that aren't named that, like "We Can Destroy EVERYTHING?!??" about Destruction Simulator ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IL67DqXrZCI)). Several videos are titled "Slide EVERYTHING Down a Hill" and show existing hill-slide games ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3hZ6ttb6ZdU), [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=d2PYiQMw_kI)). That cuts both ways. The phrase already works as a hook for creators. But on YouTube it already means riding silly objects down a ragdoll hill, not an idle money loop.

### The word promises a size ladder or a set you can finish

In every successful case, "everything" pays off in one of three ways. The first is a **finite set the player can complete**. The grocery-store game makes players eat about 4.3M calories of food, tracks **175 foods plus a hidden golden banana** in an index, and splits $1M in-game cash among teams that empty the store ([Rolimons](https://www.rolimons.com/game/128201586883489)). Destroy Everything Simulator gives you one map of 20,000+ destructible blocks ([RobloxGo](https://www.robloxgo.com/game/15855985/Destroy-Everything-Simulator)). The second is a **size ladder that ends somewhere absurd**. Katamari's 5 cm prince starts with thumbtacks and ends by rolling up buildings and clouds into a star ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katamari_Damacy)). One design analysis credits its appeal to recognisable objects and the "immediate and dramatic difference between the early game and the late game" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/analysis-how-i-katamari-i-s-scale-makes-you-high)). Tasty Planet goes from bathroom dirt to the universe ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tasty_Planet)), and Slide Down A Hill's rides go up to "a whole castle" ([Gamerant](https://gamerant.com/roblox-slide-down-a-hill-codes/)). The third is **breadth of systems**, which is Everything Upgrade Tree's approach. The version that tried literal breadth, David OReilly's *Everything*, drew the sharpest criticism: its objects were "extremely limited in function", and "a snake can't do anything a sunflower seed can't" ([EGM](https://egmnow.com/everything-review/)).

Now compare Slide Everything. Its 12 items climb in odds and value, from a 1-in-3 Toy Car to a 1-in-3,000,000 Hyperlaser, but not in size. The Diamond, Lightsaber and Hyperlaser are still hand-sized. Twelve items also don't feel like "everything", and there's no index to show the set as something to finish. On the slide, every item moves the same way at the same steered speed and differs only in what it pays. That's the *Everything* critique in miniature. The good news is that your Legendary "Slide" item, a slide sliding down a slide, already shows the absurd humour the title invites. The content just needs to keep getting bigger.

## Distance only pays when it feeds a collection, and plain slide games peak under 25K

The games where how far something goes decides the reward fall into three tiers. Two brainrot games broke 1M players, and both tie distance to a collection. Ragdoll comedy sits in the middle. Every direct "distance = cash" game found, slide games included, peaked somewhere between about 1.7K and 25K players and then faded.

|---|---|---|---|
| [Escape Tsunami For Brainrots](https://www.rolimons.com/game/131623223084840) | Run farther to grab rarer brainrots before the wave | 4,996,032 (Jan 24, 2026) | 30-day peak 40,918 at an earlier capture |
| [Kick a Lucky Block](https://www.rolimons.com/game/89469502395769) | Kick power decides where the block lands and what you get | 1,691,570 (May 23, 2026) | 30-day peak 355,146 |
| [Break your Bones](https://www.rolimons.com/game/123821081589134) | Ragdoll bone-smashing for cash and unlockable ragdolls | 56,813 (Sep 13, 2025) | 30-day peak 4,280 at capture |
| [Slide Down A Hill](https://www.rolimons.com/game/13682880624) | Ride objects down a slide and survive | 25,398 (Jul 17, 2023) | 30-day peak 60 |
| [Throwing Simulator](https://www.rolimons.com/game/90977176306153) | Throw items farther for cash | 9,639 (May 2, 2026) | 30-day peak 495 |

Kick a Lucky Block is your closest cousin. Its own description reads "Kick a lucky block to roll a brainrot", "Place collected brainrot on your plot to earn money" and "Buy weights to train your legs so you kick farther" ([Rolimons](https://www.rolimons.com/game/89469502395769)). That's your roll, slot and upgrade loop almost line for line. According to its fan wiki, landing distance picks one of **11 zones from Common to Celestial**, a timing meter rewards a "Perfect Kick" with extra reach, and the run back under a tsunami is the risk test ([kickaluckyblock.wiki](https://www.kickaluckyblock.wiki/)). One aggregator describes it differently and doesn't confirm that distance sets rarity ([Earnaldo](https://earnaldo.com/blog/break-a-lucky-block-vs-kick-a-lucky-block)), so treat that detail as likely, not certain. Three things separate it from your game. In Kick a Lucky Block, **distance feeds the collection, not just the cash**. **A fail state** creates close calls. And it has **a meme cast**. One analysis of Escape Tsunami names "close-call moments that suit streamers and YouTubers" as a driver of its breakout ([MaxPowerGaming](https://www.maxpowergaming.co/post/escape-tsunami-for-brainrots-roblox-s-next-brainrot-juggernaut)).

Slide Everything is ahead of the field in one way: **steering into multiplier walls is a skill layer most competitors lack**. They either have you watch physics (Slide Down A Hill, the ragdoll games) or throw based on stats (Throwing Simulator, Be a Car). The closest Roblox match to multiplier walls is Plinko. Ball Game added "Critical Drop, Peg Multiplier, and Multiplier Band" in July 2026 and had 0 players by October ([Rolimons](https://www.rolimons.com/game/72016039587124)), so multipliers you only watch didn't keep players. Mobile gate runners show what makes multiplier gates work. They were 3 of the top 10 US mobile games in 2022. The engaging part is choosing between gates, and each gate should cause an exaggerated visual change that reads in a five-second clip ([PocketGamer.biz](http://www.pocketgamer.biz/the-method-in-the-madness-how-to-make-a-successful-gate-runner-game/)). Your walls can only help, never hurt. They offer no choice between them, and the first two are ×1 and ×1.1, which is neither a decision nor a thumbnail.

To be honest about the niche: no 2025-2026 slide game with real player numbers turned up, and neither did any slide game with brainrots or lucky blocks. The lane is open, but that may mean low demand rather than an opportunity. The best 2026 entry in the direct-distance space, Throwing Simulator, went from 9.6K to a 30-day peak of 495. The slide won't carry the game alone. It has to be the action that drives a collection.

## The 2025-2026 hits sold a nameable set, a staged rare pull and a weekly reason to come back

The roll-and-collect family had two waves. In 2025, Grow a Garden (built in about three days) reached **22.3M concurrent players on Aug 23, 2025**. Its records landed on named update days: about 5M on May 18, 11.7M on May 31, 16M on June 14 and 21.3M on June 21 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grow_a_Garden)). Steal a Brainrot passed 25M and is the only Roblox game to do so ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steal_a_Brainrot)). In 2026, Steal An Egg reached **14.3M on Sept 19**, +1 Speed Keyboard Escape 4.0M, and Ride A Pet 1.55M ([RoStats](https://rostats.io/records)). Sol's RNG, the pure roll-for-auras game, peaked at only 193,836 in 2024 ([Rolimons](https://www.rolimons.com/game/15532962292)). But it still holds about 34.4K players and ranks #20 nearly three years on ([RoCodes](https://rocodes.gg/codes/sols-rng)). Pure rolling lasts, but it doesn't spike.

**Every hit had one coherent, nameable collectible**: brainrots, crops, eggs that hatch into pets, auras. Steal a Brainrot's characters were famous memes before the game existed ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/danidiplacido/2025/09/19/robloxs-italian-brainrot-trend-explained/)). The lesson that matters to you is that **coherence mattered more than meme status**. Grow a Garden's crops and Steal An Egg's pets aren't memes, but players read them instantly as a set with an obvious ladder. In Steal An Egg, a chicken is the usual starter and dragons come after many hours ([EGamers](https://egamers.io/robloxs-no-1-game-steal-an-egg-was-pulled-hours-after-reaching-the-top/)). The theme made these games spread, and the mechanics kept players. The Naavik podcast credits player-vs-player stealing with making Steal a Brainrot "so sticky" ([Naavik](https://naavik.co/podcast/the-future-of-fortnite-creative/)). No study compares themed collections with grab-bags directly. Still, no random-objects collection appears among the hits. Bread, Phone, D20 and Catfish are each recognisable, but together they aren't a set. They share no category and give a kid no ladder to predict. A scale ladder fixes that: "bigger means rarer" reads as instantly as chicken to dragon.

The hits also **staged their rare moments**. Steal a Brainrot puts three themed tiers above Mythic: Brainrot God, Secret and OG ([Digital Citizen](https://www.digitalcitizen.life/all-brainrots-in-steal-a-brainrot/)). Its Secret tier grew from 6 to 57 characters ([Sportskeeda](https://www.sportskeeda.com/roblox-news/all-secrets-steal-brainrot)). Sol's RNG plays a cutscene for rare rolls and announces them to the whole server ([Dot Esports](https://dotesports.com/roblox/news/what-is-the-rarest-aura-in-sols-rng)). They also **ran the game live**. Grow a Garden's "admin abuse" sessions start about an hour before an update, with weather mutations worth up to 150x ([Followchain](https://www.followchain.org/admin-abuse-grow-a-garden/)), and Plants vs Brainrots shipped weekly updates with weekend events ([MaxPowerGaming](https://www.maxpowergaming.co/post/roblox-s-latest-breakout-game-is-plants-vs-brainrots)). Your Mythics roll at 1 in 1.7M to 1 in 3M. That's great clip material, but only if the moment is staged. "Mythic" is also a generic tier name; a themed name gives kids a word to shout in a video title.

The market has also shifted. Roblox's bookings growth fell from 51% in Q2 2025 to 8% in Q2 2026 ([Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/analysis/roblox-bookings-recovery-could-decide-whether-the-stock-holds-its-rebound-200689301)). The company says players are moving away from "highly monetized viral games" ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/roblox-stock-plunges-27-weak-203853775.html)). Steal a Brainrot was down to about 129.6K concurrent players by Aug 2026 ([StudioKrew](https://studiokrew.com/blog/top-roblox-games-august-2026/)). Brainrot games shared 1.4M concurrent players across 29 tracked titles, and one February 2026 market report advised against "another pure Brainrot escape clone" because the format "peaked" ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/market-friday-an-ml-tool-that-tracks-roblox-genre-trends-%E2%80%94-heres-this-weeks-full-market-report/4413816)). Steal a Brainrot's developers have filed at least four lawsuits against imitators ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-03-24/roblox-s-hit-game-steal-a-brainrot-battles-its-many-imitators)). The game itself had to pull a character for months over a licensing dispute ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steal_a_Brainrot)). So reskinning your items as brainrots would put you in the most crowded and most litigated lane on the platform. Copy the lessons instead. The late-2026 games that still break out pair a coherent collectible with **a new physical action or stat**: +1 Speed, Kick, Ride. As one studio puts it, "evolution beats revolution" ([Dubit](https://dubit.io/library/what-makes-a-new-roblox-game-take-off)). The slide is exactly that kind of action.

## "Slide Everything" wins on grammar and loses on picture, payoff and search

Titles now matter in a specific way. Roblox's "Recommended for You" feed, which brings in most traffic, ranks games on **play-through rate first** (how many people who see the tile start playing). **First-play bounce** comes second (players leaving within 60 seconds or within 61-180 seconds), followed by play days, playtime and co-play ([Creator Hub](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/promotion/discovery)). On June 15, 2026, Roblox widened those signals from 7 to 28 days ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/recommended-for-you-algorithm-improvements-that-better-value-long-term-retention/4684575)). It did this explicitly to stop rewarding games that "win attention with exciting thumbnails" and then fail to keep players ([Dubit](https://dubit.io/library/what-makes-a-new-roblox-game-take-off)). Since July 29, 2026, **real gameplay video autoplays on recommendation tiles**, and the hand-picked Standout Games sort features video previews of new and unusual games ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/how-we-are-improving-home-this-year/4502571)). Search now matches by meaning rather than exact title words. Players mostly search hit names and broad genre words, across 50M+ searches a day ([Roblox Newsroom](https://about.roblox.com/newsroom/2025/12/roblox-replay-decoded-search-style)). Nobody types "everything" to find a game. Adding the descriptive keyword to one game's title, "Street Outlaws **Drag Racing**", got it into the top row for that search ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/searchdiscovery-suppression-despite-strong-performance/3933936)). Roblox also deprioritizes titles that look like existing games, and any metadata that implies a money reward ([Creator Hub](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/promotion/discovery)).

Measured against that, the current title has the right grammar. It's an imperative verb plus an object, two words long, in the style of Grow a Garden and Steal a Brainrot, games that share little "beyond their short and snappy titles" ([Screen Rant](https://screenrant.com/roblox-steal-brainrot-fad-explanation/)). It's also ownable, since no Roblox game with this name turned up, and it fits any item you add later. It has four problems, though. First, it's unclear who slides: on Roblox, "slide" usually means your avatar slides, and the YouTube "Slide EVERYTHING Down a Hill" videos reinforce that reading. Second, you can't draw "everything" on an icon. Third, it says nothing about money, multipliers or rolling. Fourth, it promises more than 12 items deliver in the first minute, which feeds bounce. On a weighted scorecard that counts the click and the bounce triple, it scores **46 of 85**. The scores in the table below are my own untested judgments, not measured data.

| Title | One-line hook | What it gains | What it costs | Score /85 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Slide Everything (as is) | none | Brand, room to grow | Can't be pictured, no payoff, no search word | 46 |
| Slide Everything + fixed tile and description | "Roll rare items, slide them through multiplier walls, get paid for every stud" | Semantic search reads the description; the tile shows items sliding | Title still unclear on who slides | ~50-52 |
| Slide Everything + a delivered scale ladder | "Start with a toy car. End by sliding the sun." | Planet-on-a-slide is drawable; first minute is an honest start of the climb | Needs new content before launch | ~60 (my estimate) |
| Roll & Slide | "Roll rare items, send them down the slide. The farther they go, the richer you get." | Names both halves of the loop; "roll" hints at RNG | No concrete noun; collisions unchecked | ~60 |
| Slide RNG | "Roll for 1-in-a-million items, then slide them through ×10 walls." | Genre word players type | Generic, crowded, forgettable | ~58 |
| Send It Down the Slide | "Roll an item. Send it. Smash the multipliers." | Very sayable; item-as-object is clear | Five words; weak search word | ~60 |

So the rule is this. **If you build the scale ladder below, keep "Slide Everything"**: it then scores about as well as the best rename while keeping its distinctiveness and the jokes built into your item list. **If you won't build it, rename to "Roll & Slide" before any marketing push.** Either way, settle the core name once, before launch. After that, only decorate it with a single update tag or emoji, the way Fish It! cycles "[UPD]" and "[3 HRS]" prefixes around a fixed name ([RoWatcher](https://rowatcher.com/games/6701277882)). Avoid "Slide a Brainrot" and avoid reward language like "free Robux".

Whatever the title, the tile and metadata need four fixes. **Make the description's first sentence name the loop**, for example: "Roll rare items, slide them through multiplier walls, and get paid for every stud." That puts "roll", "slide" and "multiplier" where search reads them. **Turn on thumbnail personalization with four or five genuinely different concepts.** Roblox reports an average **+8.5% lift in qualified play-through**, with some games gaining 50% ([Creator Hub](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/publishing/thumbnails)). Staff report +12% on average across 8,000+ games and advise against near-identical variants ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/5-tips-from-roblox-staff-to-get-the-most-out-of-thumbnail-personalization/3471689)). Concepts to try: an item smashing a big multiplier wall, a catfish speeding down the slide, a "1 in 3,000,000" roll reveal, glowing full slots, and later the sun on the slide. **Cut the gameplay video to the 3-5 seconds where items cross walls while the stud counter climbs.** It must be real gameplay, with no cinematic trailer. **Ask a few kids your target age "what do you do in this game?"** after showing them only the tile, and type each candidate title into Roblox search to check for collisions.

Two housekeeping items. First, your public GitHub repo is the top web result for the game's name, and clones now appear within days, some built with AI ([Game Rant](https://gamerant.com/escape-the-walls-roblox-clones/)). Consider making the repo private before launch. Second, "Lightsaber" is a Star Wars term. The research didn't verify the trademark risk, but swapping it for something generic like "Plasma Blade" before it appears on a tile costs nothing.

## Twelve changes that make the title true without starting over

Every change below builds on systems you already have: the Items.luau table, the Rarities.luau tiers and gradients, the roll stands, the Stage1/Stage2 slide sections that the Slide upgrade extends, the multiplier walls with their Multiplier attribute, Drops.STUD_SECONDS, and steering. They're ordered from "keep" through "change" to "try". The evidence column says where each idea comes from, and the ones marked inference haven't been tested on Roblox.

| # | Verdict | Change | In your game | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Keep | The core loop: roll stands, slots for passive income, steering the slide, per-stud pay, upgrades | Don't restart. This is Kick a Lucky Block's loop with a skill layer it lacks | Kick a Lucky Block's 1.69M peak; steering is rare in the niche |
| 2 | Change | **Turn rarity into size** | Keep the 12 items as the "pocket-size" chapter. Add rarer chapters that get physically bigger: room-sized (fridge, couch, arcade cabinet), street-sized (car, bus, shipping container), city-sized (house, Ferris wheel, skyscraper), then space (moon, planet, the sun). Sell the size with a camera pull-back when big items drop | Katamari, Tasty Planet, Slide Down A Hill's "whole castle"; size ladders are how "everything" pays off |
| 3 | Change | **Make each new slide section bigger in scenery** | As the Slide upgrade adds sections past Stage2, theme them bedroom, then street, then city, then sky, then space, so going farther literally passes through everything | Inference from the scale-ladder analogues; cheap because the sections already exist |
| 4 | Change | **Add a Slide Index** | A collection book showing every item and mutation as silhouettes, marked "slid / not yet slid", with a first-slide bonus, a completion badge and one hidden secret item | The grocery game's 175-food index and hidden golden banana; makes "everything" finite and visible |
| 5 | Change | **Add themed tiers above Mythic, plus mutations** | Add a "Cosmic"-style tier to Rarities.luau for the space chapter. Add Golden, Rainbow and Cosmic mutations on top of rolls with their own odds and income multiplier. That turns 12 models into 48 index entries without new meshes | Steal a Brainrot's Brainrot God, Secret and OG tiers; Grow a Garden's weather mutations |
| 6 | Change | **Use fewer, bigger, louder walls** | Replace ×1 and ×1.1 steps with ×2, ×5 and ×10 walls. Each should visibly change the item (it grows, catches fire, turns rainbow). Lower Drops.STUD_SECONDS so total pay per drop stays where the economy sheet wants it | Gate-runner advice: an exaggerated visual change per gate makes the clip |
| 7 | Change | **Stage rare rolls and rare slides** | A short cutscene, a distinct sound and a server-wide announcement for rolls rarer than about 1 in 100,000, plus a first-in-server badge. Announce and spotlight it when someone slides a Mythic | Sol's RNG cutscenes and server-wide announcements |
| 8 | Change | **Rename Lightsaber** | Use a generic name like "Plasma Blade" in Items.luau before any thumbnail shows it | Avoids a Star Wars term (trademark risk not verified) |
| 9 | Try | **Give the slide choices and risk** | Split the slide before a wall into a safe ×2 lane and a risky ×5 lane with a gap or bumpers. Items knocked off stop earning for that run but never leave their slot. Add a "Perfect Drop" timing meter at release for a speed boost | Kick a Lucky Block's Perfect Kick and tsunami; gate runners' choice of gate |
| 10 | Try | **Let distance feed the rolls** | Crossing the end of each slide section grants a luck boost or a free roll at that section's tier, so sliding farther improves the collection as well as paying cash | Kick a Lucky Block's distance-to-zone tiers (fan wiki) |
| 11 | Try | **Add a shared, public layer** | Make rare drops visible to the whole server. Add an hourly "longest slide" and "biggest drop" leaderboard, timed roll-stand restocks with limited items, and "slide storm" events (all walls doubled for 10 minutes) run live on update days | Admin-abuse events and timed stock in Grow a Garden and Plants vs Brainrots |
| 12 | Try | **Ship content every week** | A small drop each week (two or three items, or one mutation) plus one timed event. Each new item is a model, an icon and one entry in Items.luau | Records landing on named update days; the 28-day retention signals |

Do these in a sensible order. **Before launch**, do the cheap fixes that protect click-through and the first minute: the title decision, the description sentence, thumbnails and video, the Lightsaber rename, staged reveals (7), and the Slide Index with the current 12 items (4). **The first content push** should build the identity: the scale ladder, the escalating slide sections and the themed top tier (2, 3, 5). Make the sun a goal players can see from day one, even locked. **Then work on retention**: risk lanes, distance feeding rolls and the shared layer (9-11), with the weekly cadence (12) once players exist. Use Creator Analytics to read the result. First-play bounce under 60 seconds tells you whether the title over-promises. Play-through rate tells you whether the tile works. Day-1 and day-7 return rates tell you whether the slide is fun the twentieth time.

## Conclusion

You're right to doubt "everything", but the problem isn't that the word is vague. It only becomes a liability while the game under it is a dozen hand-sized objects that all slide the same way. Treat it as a design brief instead of a label. Escalate the scale until the player slides the sun, and make the set finite and visible. Then the word becomes your thumbnail and your progression at once, and the Slide-length upgrade you already planned becomes the way players climb it. Your real edge is something no slide-genre competitor has: the item is both the thing you collect and the thing you launch, and the player steers it.

Set your ambition to match the evidence. The comparable games say a well-made version lands in the low thousands of peak players and lives on retention. Everything Upgrade Tree is the model: an 8.7K peak, 23-minute sessions, and still played almost two years on ([Rolimons](https://www.rolimons.com/game/122809141833750)). It got there without a viral spike. Roblox's current algorithm favours exactly that shape, since it ranks per-user engagement and states that "smaller experiences with highly engaged users are not disadvantaged" ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/boost-your-discovery-with-the-improved-recommended-for-you-algorithm-and-analytics-for-creators/3587441)). Steady depth and weekly updates are what a solo developer can actually deliver. Spikes like Kick a Lucky Block's came with a meme cast and a live-operations team behind them, and chasing them now with a brainrot reskin would mean entering the platform's most crowded lane at its decline.
