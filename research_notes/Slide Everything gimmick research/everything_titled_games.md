# "Everything"-titled Roblox games and what the word "everything" promises

Research date: 2026-10-23. All Rolimons figures below were fetched on 2026-10-23. Rolimons gives some dates only as relative times ("2 weeks ago"), and those are converted to approximate calendar dates here. Third-party trackers often disagree; where they do, both figures are given.

## Q1. Which Roblox games use "Everything" in the title, and how did they perform?

### Takeaway
Many Roblox games use "Everything" in the title, but most stay small or spike and collapse. Only one is a real breakout right now: **Eat Everything In A Grocery Store** (Oct 2026, about 18.7K peak CCU). It rode MrBeast's real-life "Eat Everything In A Grocery Store" video; the word did not carry it. The biggest games built on the same "consume the world" fantasy (Eat the World, 634M visits; Slide Down A Hill, 199M) do **not** use the word "everything". None of the exact titles in the brief turned up: Merge, Catch, Grow, Fling, Kick, Sell, Break, Lift or Throw Everything.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- The word "everything" does not reliably drive success on Roblox. The two biggest hits on this fantasy (Eat the World at 52.6K peak; Slide Down A Hill at 25.4K peak, see Q2) leave it out of the title. The one current "everything" breakout borrows its full title from a record-breaking MrBeast video.
- Trajectories split three ways:
  1. Trend-jack spikes, e.g. Grocery Store, which is still rising.
  2. Spike-and-crash: Everything Factory went from 7,360 to a 30-day peak of 35 within ~4 months; Vacuum Everything went from 1,565 to 6.
  3. One slow, durable incremental, Everything Upgrade Tree: 23-minute sessions and still 100-400 CCU almost two years in.
- Everything Upgrade Tree is the closest genre match to Slide Everything (an idle/incremental game). Its "everything" refers to breadth of systems, not physical scale.

### Gaps
- Many smaller "X Everything" games surely exist but are not indexed by web search. Roblox's own search would surface more, but it could not be queried here.
- No peak-CCU history was found for Eat Everything (Chengdu Clover) or Eat Everything Simulator (Habit Games).
- No reliable data on whether "Everything" in a title changes click-through on Roblox's discovery surfaces.

## Q2. Is there already a Roblox game called "Slide Everything", or something very close?

### Takeaway
No Roblox experience titled "Slide Everything" turned up. The exact phrase mostly brings up (a) the developer's own public GitHub repo and (b) several YouTube videos titled "Slide EVERYTHING Down a Hill", which cover existing hill-slide games. The main search competitor is **Slide Down A Hill** (BaCkaw): 199M visits and 25.4K peak CCU in July 2023, now at about 17-60 CCU. Its fantasy is riding ever-sillier objects (up to "a whole castle") down a giant slide.

One is described as sliding objects down a hill for in-game money — [YouTube 1](https://m.youtube.com/watch?v=csvvKikUw3Q), [YouTube 2](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=d2PYiQMw_kI)
  - "Slide EVERYTHING Down a Hill in Roblox!" — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3hZ6ttb6ZdU)
  - "Roblox Slide EVERYTHING Down a HILL!!" — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=x9qpFFrKxhI)

- The developer's GitHub repo is public and is the top search hit for the name (an observation, not a recommendation).

- Slide House Tumble's stats were not fetched.

## Q3. How much content did the successful "everything" games ship, and is scale escalation the hook?

### Takeaway
Successful "everything" games deliver the word in one of three ways:
1. **A bounded, completable "everything"**: a whole store (175 foods, 4.3M calories), a whole map (20,000+ blocks), or every object in a level (Donut County).
2. **Escalating scale**: Katamari runs from thumbtacks to stars, and Tasty Planet runs from bathroom dirt to the universe.
3. **Breadth of systems**: Everything Upgrade Tree keeps adding new mechanics.

Physical escalation from small to huge is the proven hook in the eat/roll/hole family. A rarity ladder of hand-sized objects (Slide Everything's current 12 items) does not match any of the three.

### Cited Findings
- **Eat Everything In A Grocery Store**:
  - About 4.3 million calories of food, all of which must be eaten; items break apart as you bite them, including a 150,000-calorie giant watermelon.
  - A food index tracks **175 foods** plus a hidden golden banana.
  - Gym upgrades: hold-to-eat, eating speed, multi-eat (up to 12 items at once), calorie gain and reach.
  - Teams that empty the store split $1,000,000 in-game cash, and leaderboards track the fastest store clears.
  - [Rolimons description](https://www.rolimons.com/game/128201586883489)
- The real MrBeast challenge behind it lasted 202 days. About 4.3M calories had to be cleared, and only 6,614 calories were wasted — [Dexerto](https://www.dexerto.com/youtube/mrbeast-breaks-his-youtube-views-record-with-1-million-grocery-store-challenge-3415556/)
- **Eat the World**: "Eat the world, one chunk at a time!" You grow bigger by eating terrain chunks, buy upgrades, throw dirt at others, and play timed maps that can be skipped — [Rolimons](https://www.rolimons.com/game/16480898254); [NamuWiki](https://en.namu.wiki/w/Eat%20the%20World)
- **Destroy Everything Simulator**: one map with 20,000+ destructible blocks and a hidden "Destroy Everything" button; regen buttons reset the map — [RobloxGo](https://www.robloxgo.com/game/15855985/Destroy-Everything-Simulator)
- **Everything Upgrade Tree**: an "incremental idle game that expands as you progress", with Cards, Astronomy, Mining and a "literal brainrot" section — [Rolimons](https://www.rolimons.com/game/122809141833750)
- **The Everything Factory**: tycoon where rare items pass by on a conveyor; drop and upgrade ores, roll for mutations, auto-build, rebirths, offline earnings. The item count was not given — [Rolimons](https://www.rolimons.com/game/96033111684913)
- **Vacuum Everything**: vacuum through blocks, dig for loot, crates for rare skins — [Rolimons](https://www.rolimons.com/game/84402061711337)
- **Slide Down A Hill**: biggest ride example is "a whole castle"; ride count not specified — [Gamerant](https://gamerant.com/roblox-slide-down-a-hill-codes/)
- **Katamari Damacy**:
  - The Prince is 5 cm tall. You start rolling thumbtacks and ants and end with buildings, mountains and clouds until the katamari becomes a star. The premise is replacing stars the King of All Cosmos destroyed.
  - MoMA praised its "quirky manipulations of scale"; the cover shows a katamari about to roll over a city.
  - Metacritic 86; won Excellence in Game Design at the 2005 GDCAs.
  - Criticised as short and repetitive (under 10 hours).
  - [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katamari_Damacy)
- Design analyses credit Katamari's appeal to:
  - Recognisable objects, which make the scale change concrete, and an "immediate and dramatic difference between the early game and the late game" — Gregory Weir, 2009, [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/analysis-how-i-katamari-i-s-scale-makes-you-high)
  - Obstacles turning into collectibles as you grow, and camera/FOV changes that sell the scale — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/analysis-how-i-katamari-i-s-scale-makes-you-high); [Natasha Tylosky, 2026](https://www.natashatylosky.com/tashas-design-corner/2026/6/19/the-brilliant-interaction-design-of-katamari-damacy)
  - A "nested tree of different kinds of progression" — [Unwinnable, 2019](https://unwinnable.com/2019/01/23/katamri-damacy-keep-going/)
- **Tasty Planet** (Dingo Games, 2006): grey goo starts eating bathroom dirt, then consumes the Solar System and galaxies, and finally implodes and recreates the universe. 60 levels in 9 chapters — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tasty_Planet)

### Inferences
- In every successful case, "everything" is either a **finite set the player can finish** (empty the store, clear the level, wreck the map) or a **visible size ladder** (ant to star). Players read the word as a promise of one or both.
- Slide Everything's 12 items (Toy Car through Hyperlaser) climb in rarity and value, not in physical size: a Diamond and a Lightsaber are still hand-sized. The genre's absurd-scale payoff would mean sliding a house, a ship, a planet or the sun, and nothing in the current list does that. Slide Down A Hill already reaches "a whole castle".
- The Grocery Store game's 175 foods and Everything Upgrade Tree's ever-widening system list suggest "everything" games need either a large catalogue (well over 12 items) or a "collect them all" index that makes the finite set visible.

### Gaps
- No item counts were found for Eat Everything (Chengdu Clover), Eat Everything Simulator, The Everything Factory, or Slide Down A Hill's ride catalogue.
- No Roblox-specific data was found on whether scale escalation improves retention in idle games, as opposed to action games.

## Q4. What do players and developers say about "everything" titles?

### Takeaway
No Reddit or DevForum discussion specifically about the word "everything" in titles turned up, so this is a real evidence gap. The general naming advice that does exist warns about crowded, generic and look-alike titles and says gameplay matters more once players arrive. The clearest signal is behavioural: YouTubers put "EVERYTHING" in capitals in their video titles even when the game isn't named that. That makes the word a thumbnail and title hook for creators more than a game-name asset.

### Cited Findings
- DevForum naming advice (Game Design Support threads, mostly 2020-2025; exact dates not captured):
  - Search Roblox for your candidate name; if popular games already use it, "you may be accused of copying".
  - Simulators are crowded: "there are too many simulators out there, and they all die eventually".
  - Including "Simulator" skews the audience younger.
  - "Once you have a lot of players, the gameplay matters more than the name."
  - A personal checklist: "is this name easy to remember? Is it similar to another? ... does this sound like a game I want to play?"
  - [DevForum: Advice on naming a game](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/advice-on-naming-a-game/1989821); [DevForum: Should I call my game a simulator](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/should-i-call-my-game-a-simulator-or-not/2253714); [DevForum: Will having simulator in the title...](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/will-having-simulator-in-the-title-of-my-game-make-people-write-it-off-before-they-give-it-a-chance/946434); [DevForum: Feedback on game name](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/feedback-on-game-name/1652941)
- DevForum, 2025: a developer reports a group copying their "thumbnail, description, and even our title, basically copying everything 1:1". Title cloning is a live problem on the platform — [DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/another-game-copied-our-thumbnail-title-description-and-map/3905142)
- An itch.io commenter (non-Roblox) complained about a "super generic name that's impossible to search" — [itch.io](https://itch.io/post/5824802)
- Creators use "EVERYTHING" as a video-title hook for games not named that:
  - "We Can Destroy EVERYTHING?!?? - Roblox Destruction Simulator" — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IL67DqXrZCI)
  - "We Slide EVERYTHING Down A Hill In Roblox!" — [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=d2PYiQMw_kI)
- Ratings among "Everything" titles track depth, not the word:
  - Higher: The Everything Factory 92.6%, Eat Everything In A Grocery Store 88.6%, Everything Upgrade Tree 87.5%.
  - Lower: Vacuum Everything 70.2%, Poop Everything 63.8%.
  - [Rolimons pages cited in Q1](https://www.rolimons.com/game/96033111684913)
- Outside Roblox, critics of David OReilly's *Everything* praised its ideas but found the objects "extremely limited in function", saying "a snake can't do anything a sunflower seed can't" — [EGM review](https://egmnow.com/everything-review/)
- Perception of Roblox copycats in general: in Aug 2025, PEAK's developers called a Roblox clone a "microtransaction-riddled Roblox slop ripoff" — [Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Steam-indie-bestseller-PEAK-s-developer-recommends-pirating-the-game-instead-of-playing-a-Roblox-ripoff.1080200.0.html)

### Inferences
- There is no evidence that players punish "everything" titles as clickbait as such. The risk is indirect: a generic word gets lost in search, and a big promise invites the *Everything*-style complaint that the many things all play the same.
- Since creators already put "EVERYTHING" in video titles, a game whose name works naturally in a video title ("We Slide EVERYTHING...") may help creator coverage. This is speculative.

### Gaps
- No r/roblox or r/robloxgamedev threads on "everything" titles were found in searches. YouTube comment sentiment could not be retrieved.
- No A/B or CTR data on title wording for Roblox was found.

## Q5. How have "everything" concepts worked outside Roblox, and what did "everything" mean in practice?

### Takeaway
Outside Roblox, "everything" has worked when it means **escalating consumption with a payoff at planetary or cosmic scale** (Katamari, Tasty Planet, Hole.io's whole city), or **clearing every object in a bounded space** (Donut County). Literal "everything" breadth (David OReilly's *Everything*, 3,000+ things) earned critical respect, but its objects were shallow. The hypercasual "eat/grow everything" hit Hole.io won through a one-swipe size ladder plus heavy ad spend.

### Cited Findings
- **Everything** (David OReilly, 2017):
  - "over 3000 different things if you include variations" (Hypebeast interview); EGM describes "hundreds" of objects.
  - Steam: 84% positive of 2,318 English reviews; Metacritic 78.
  - Sales were not found.
  - [Hypebeast](https://hypebeast.com/2017/4/david-oreilly-everything-video-game-interview); [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/582270/Everything/); [EGM](https://egmnow.com/everything-review/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Everything_(video_game))
- **Katamari Damacy** (2004):
  - Sales: top seller in Japan in its launch week (32,000 units), 155,000+ in Japan by end of 2004 (below Namco's 500K estimate), 120,000+ in North America.
  - Its scale runs from thumbtacks to a star, as covered in Q3.
  - [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katamari_Damacy)
- **Donut County** (Ben Esposito, 2018):
  - A hole swallows every object in a level to clear it, consuming "the homes and residents of Donut County".
  - Metacritic 75-87 depending on platform; Apple's iPhone Game of the Year 2018; SXSW Mobile Game of the Year 2019.
  - GamesRadar+'s review title: "Just like a real donut, it's finished far too quickly".
  - [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donut_County)
- **Hole.io** (Voodoo, 2018):
  - Swipe to grow a black hole and swallow a city.
  - About 420-430M Google Play installs (AppBrain; third-party estimate).
  - Hit #1 in free apps at launch and ranked above Fortnite. Variety credits mass, cheap ad buys.
  - Esposito publicly objected that it cloned Donut County, which was still unreleased.
  - [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hole.io); [AppBrain](https://www.appbrain.com/app/hole-io/io.voodoo.holeio); [Variety](https://variety.com/2018/gaming/features/donut-county-hole-io-apple-1202866615/); [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/clever-indie-game-donut-county-isnt-even-out-yet-and-al-1827117824)
- **Tasty Planet** (Dingo Games, 2006): grey goo eats from dirt up to the Solar System and beyond, then "destroys and recreates the universe"; 60 levels; sequels in 2011/2016 (Back for Seconds) and Tasty Planet Forever — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tasty_Planet)
- **Real-world "everything" spectacle**: MrBeast's "Eat Everything In A Grocery Store" works because the set is finite and the progress is measurable (4.3M calories counting down over 202 days). It drew a billion+ minutes of watch time, with average viewers staying 20+ minutes — [Dexerto](https://www.dexerto.com/youtube/mrbeast-breaks-his-youtube-views-record-with-1-million-grocery-store-challenge-3415556/)

### Inferences
- Across media, "everything" pays off through either (1) a countdown to a finished set or (2) a size ladder that ends at absurd scale (planet, star, universe). The literal-breadth version (*Everything*) was the least commercially obvious of these and drew the "shallow objects" critique.
- For a slide-based idle game, the closest equivalent would be the objects themselves growing in physical scale along the rarity ladder (toy, then car, house, ship, mountain, planet, sun), and/or a visible "slid 12/12 items" or "index" completion goal. Both are inferences from the analogues, not tested on Roblox.

### Gaps
- No sales figures were found for David OReilly's *Everything*, and no unit sales for Donut County.
- No hypercasual market write-up (e.g. from Voodoo, Sensor Tower or GameAnalytics) specifically on "eat/grow/merge everything" retention or CPI was found.
